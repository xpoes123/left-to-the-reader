# Puzzle 003 — "The Cartographer's Compass"

- **Source:** Symmetric-Lights-Out / paired-toggle family. Many olympiad
  problems use "opposite pair" coupling.
- **Underlying idea:** Geometric/structural invariance. The mechanic
  *physically locks* opposite cells together — they always change in sync.
  The puzzle has not one invariant but *four* (one per opposite pair).
- **Visual surface:** A round compass face engraved in tarnished copper,
  divided into 8 sectors by thin radial lines. Each sector shows either a
  tiny etched sun or a tiny crescent moon. The compass sits on a navigator's
  table, half-illuminated.
- **Verbs:**
  - Click any sector → that sector AND its diametrically opposite sector
    both toggle (sun↔moon).
- **Starting state** (clockwise from N): sun, moon, sun, sun, sun, moon,
  sun, sun.
- **Goal state:** all eight sectors show suns.
- **The aha:** The player will likely click around for a bit before noticing
  that opposite sectors *always change together*. Once that lands, they
  stop thinking in 8 cells and start thinking in 4 pairs. The puzzle solves
  itself once the player makes that mental shift.
- **Predecessor artifact** (in the book's inside cover, in H.M.'s hand):
  > *"The compass remembers in pairs. The north star will always shadow its
  > other. I lost a year arguing with this. — H.M."*
- **Partner artifact:** none (Act 1).
- **Difficulty tier:** Tutorial → Easy. ~5 min once the pair-locking is
  felt; longer if the player resists.
- **Act:** 1
- **Notes:**
  - This is the first puzzle with a *geometric* invariant rather than a
    counted / numerical one — a new flavor. "Look for what doesn't change"
    now means "look for what's *coupled*."
  - The artifact's "I lost a year arguing with this" is the first hint that
    H.M. was sometimes *wrong* — they tried to defeat the invariant before
    accepting it. The player retracing their steps eventually realizes
    H.M. eventually saw it too. This humanizes H.M. as a thinker, not an
    oracle.
